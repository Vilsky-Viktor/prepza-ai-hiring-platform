# How to screen 100 applicants in a day

A job ad goes out and 100 applications come in. Reading each CV carefully takes time you don't have, so in practice the first 20 get attention and the rest get a skim. That isn't fair to applicants, and it isn't good for you: a strong applicant may be number 87.

This guide lays out a one-day plan that gives every applicant the same chance and gets you to a short list by the evening. It uses a short skills test as the main filter, with people making every decision.

## The plan at a glance

| Time | Step | Your effort |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Morning, 30 minutes | Decide what the job needs and write the must-haves down | Focused thinking |
| Morning, 30 minutes | Create a short skills test from the job description and review it | Reviewing topics, previewing the test |
| Late morning, 15 minutes | Check hard requirements and invite everyone who meets them | A quick pass, not a deep read |
| During the day | Candidates take the test | None |
| Afternoon, 1–2 hours | Review the top results, look at answers and flags | Careful reading of a small group |
| End of day | Pick the short list for interviews | A decision, made by people |

The test does the sorting. Your attention goes to the part that needs judgment.

## Step 1: Decide what the job needs (30 minutes)

Before you test anyone, write down what a person in this role must know on day one. Be specific.

- **Knowledge:** "Reconciles bank statements, knows VAT basics, uses a general ledger" is testable. "Strong finance background" isn't.
- **Hard requirements:** a license, a work permit, a language level, availability. These are yes-or-no checks, not test material.
- **Nice to have:** keep these out of the first filter. They belong in the interview.

If two or three people are involved in the hire, agree on this list now. It's much harder to agree after you've seen the candidates.

## Step 2: Create a short skills test (30 minutes)

A good screening test is short, job-related and the same for everyone.

With prepza:

1. **Paste the job description.** prepza picks out what a candidate must know and proposes topics.
2. **Review the topics against your list from step 1.** Uncheck anything that isn't a real requirement, rename topics, edit subtopics, or describe changes in plain text ("add a topic on payroll basics"). Questions are written only after you approve.
3. **Set the length.** By default each topic asks 10 questions, each with a 60-second countdown, or 75 seconds on a hard test. Three topics means at most 30 minutes for a candidate (about 38 on a hard test), and usually less. Keep it to what you need.
4. **Set a pass mark.** The default is 70%. Treat it as a guide for reading results, not an automatic cut.
5. **Preview it as a candidate.** Take the test yourself. It's free and doesn't count as a candidate. If a question looks off, re-generate it.

If you hire for a common role, you can start from a ready-made template instead and adjust it.

### Why a short knowledge test works as a first filter

- **It's the same for everyone.** Same topics, same number of questions, same time limit.
- **It's harder to game.** Each candidate gets their own random questions, with a timer on each one.
- **It's job-related.** Every topic comes from the job description you approved.
- **It's quick to read.** Results come in ranked by score.

Large reviews of hiring research rank job-specific knowledge tests among the better predictors of job performance on average, and years of experience among the weaker ones. See [Skills tests vs CV screening](/guides/skills-tests-vs-cv-screening).

## Step 3: Check hard requirements, then invite (15 minutes)

Do a fast yes-or-no pass on the hard requirements only: right to work, required license, location if the role needs it. Don't rank CVs here. You're removing applicants who can't take the job, not choosing favorites.

Then invite everyone left:

- **Paste a list of emails or upload a file.** prepza takes up to 100 emails at once and skips anyone who already started.
- **Or use one link.** If you haven't posted the job yet, put the interview's shareable link in the ad. Applicants can take the test right away and appear in your list, ranked. Turn the link off once the role is filled.
- **Or let your ATS send them.** If your applicants are in Workable, Greenhouse, Teamtailor, Recruitee or Breezy HR, connect it once: moving a candidate to a stage sends the invite, and results go back to the ATS. See [how to connect skills tests to your ATS](/guides/ats-integration-skills-tests).

Before they start, candidates see what to expect: the timer, that a person at your company reviews results, and that they can ask for more time. If someone asks for an accommodation, you can add extra time for that candidate before they begin.

### Getting tests done the same day

A clear, short test is easier to finish soon after the invite. Say in your email or job ad how long it takes and when you need it by. prepza sends a reminder automatically to anyone who hasn't started after 3 days; for a same-day plan, you can also resend an invite yourself.

Not everyone will finish on day one. That's fine: review whoever has finished and look again at the stragglers the next morning.

## Step 4: Review the results (1–2 hours)

Now the careful reading starts, but on a smaller, ranked group.

1. **Sort by grade,** best first, from the list's sort menu: the list opens with the newest candidates first.
2. **Open each scorecard** in the top group. Look at the answers, not just the number: which topics were strong, which were weak, how long each answer took.
3. **Check the integrity flags.** prepza flags answers too fast to have read the question, times the candidate left the page, and copy attempts. A flag is a reason to look closer, not proof of cheating. Someone who left the page once might have had a notification pop up.
4. **Look just below the pass mark.** Scores from different random question sets aren't exactly comparable, so a few points can come from the draw. Someone at 65% with strong answers in the topics that matter most may be worth an interview.
5. **Now read the CVs** of the people you're considering. You're reading 15 CVs carefully instead of skimming 100.

To share results with a hiring manager, download a PDF report for one candidate or the whole list, or email it from prepza.

## Step 5: Decide (end of day)

Pick your short list. A person makes this decision, using the test as one input alongside the CV and anything else you know. Don't reject anyone on the score alone without looking at their answers.

Write down why each person made the list. It takes a minute per candidate and helps if anyone asks later.

## Common mistakes

- **Testing for "nice to have."** Every extra topic makes the test longer and filters out people for things you could teach.
- **A test that's too long.** Long tests lose good candidates who have other offers.
- **Treating the pass mark as a wall.** It's a reading aid. Look at the answers.
- **Skipping the preview.** Take the test yourself before candidates do.
- **Ignoring accommodations.** Offer extra time to anyone who asks.
