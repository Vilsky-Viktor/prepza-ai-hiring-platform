# How to compare pre-employment test costs

Which pre-employment test costs least depends on one number: how many candidates you test in a year. A monthly plan that looks cheap can cost more per candidate than a pay-as-you-go option if you only hire a few times a year. A per-candidate price that looks high can cost less at low volume.

This page doesn't list other vendors' prices, because they change often. It explains the pricing models, shows how to estimate your own cost from any vendor's pricing page, and lists what the price doesn't include. The tools also test different things, and some teams use more than one, so cost is only part of the choice.

## How to estimate your cost

You need three numbers: how many candidates you test in a year, the price per candidate on a pay-per-candidate option, and the yearly cost of a plan.

1. **Count your candidates.** Roles you hire in a year × applicants you'd test per role. Be realistic: count the people who'd actually take the test.
2. **Pay per candidate:** candidates × price per candidate. Take off any free candidates.
3. **Subscription:** the monthly price × 12, or the annual price. Check how many candidates its credits cover; if you'd go over, price the next plan up.
4. **Find your break-even point:** the plan's yearly cost ÷ the price per candidate. Below that many candidates a year, paying per candidate costs less. Above it, the plan does.

**A worked example, with made-up numbers.** Say a plan costs $1,500 a year and a pay-per-candidate option costs $5 a candidate. The break-even point is 1,500 ÷ 5 = 300 candidates a year. A team testing 60 candidates a year would pay $300 in per-candidate fees instead of $1,500 for the plan. A team testing 600 would pay $3,000 in per-candidate fees, and the plan would cost less. Use the real numbers from the pricing pages you're comparing.

## Costs beyond the sticker price

When you compare, check these too:

- **Annual commitment.** A plan "billed annually" means paying for 12 months up front, even if you only hire in two of them.
- **Seats.** Some plans limit how many people on your team can use them. prepza has no seat fees: owners, admins and viewers are free.
- **Candidates who never start.** prepza charges only for candidates who answer at least one question; an unused invite's credits come back.
- **Expiring credits.** prepza's credits don't expire. Check whether other vendors' credits roll over.
- **Taxes.** Check whether a listed price includes VAT or sales tax.
- **Features behind higher plans.** ATS integrations, proctoring and custom coding are often on more expensive plans. Check which plan you'd actually need. prepza connects to Workable, Greenhouse, Teamtailor, Recruitee, Breezy HR and Slack, and includes its API, at no extra cost.
- **More than one tool.** If you need a knowledge test and a psychometric or coding test, add up both.

## When a lower price isn't the right choice

A low price only helps if the test measures what your job needs. Pay for another tool, alone or next to prepza, when:

- **You need psychometrics.** Validated cognitive and personality tests come from specialist vendors and broader platforms.
- **You need hands-on coding.** Developer platforms let candidates write and run code. prepza asks code-reading questions (what code does or outputs), but candidates don't write or run code.
- **You need video or AI-led interviews.** prepza has neither.
- **You hire at steady high volume.** A flat plan can cost less than a per-candidate price.
