# testee and TestGorilla: where each fits, and using both

It's natural to compare testee and TestGorilla, but the two aren't really rivals. TestGorilla offers a large library of ready-made tests, including cognitive, personality, language and programming tests. testee does one thing: it writes a timed multiple-choice knowledge interview from your job description. Many hiring processes could use both, for example testee as a quick, role-specific knowledge screen and TestGorilla for cognitive or personality tests on the short list.

This page explains how they differ, where each one fits better, and how to combine them. TestGorilla facts come from its own pages as of October 2026 and can change; check the links before you decide.

## The short version

- **TestGorilla fits** when you need cognitive ability, personality, language or hands-on coding tests, video questions or webcam snapshots.
- **testee fits** when you want a knowledge test written from your exact job description, including code-reading questions for technical roles, and you'd rather pay per candidate than for a plan.
- **Both fit** when you want a role-specific knowledge screen for every applicant and broader tests for the people who pass it.

## How tests are built

**TestGorilla** starts from its library. You pick tests that fit the role (for example a role-specific skills test plus a cognitive test plus a personality test) and combine them into an assessment. Its library is organized into categories such as role-specific skills, programming skills, software skills, language, cognitive ability, situational judgment, personality and culture, and typing ([test library](https://www.testgorilla.com/test-library/)).

**testee** starts from your job description. It picks out what a candidate must know and proposes topics with subtopics. You keep, uncheck, rename or edit them, or describe changes in plain text. Only then are the questions written: a bank of multiple-choice questions per topic, most with one correct answer and three plausible wrong ones, and about 1 in 10 "select all that apply", with 2 or 3 correct answers. You can see every question and re-generate any of them. For technical topics, many questions show a short code example, query or command and ask what it outputs, what it does, why it fails or which change fixes it.

The practical difference: a library test is fixed and has been used many times, but it isn't written for your role. A testee interview is written for your role, including its niche parts, but it's new each time. AI-written questions can contain mistakes, so testee checks a sample of each topic's answer keys before release and the rest when a question is flagged, improves questions as candidates answer, and lets you flag any question.

## How pricing works

**TestGorilla** offers a free plan with a monthly allowance and paid plans billed annually, with more features on higher plans. See its [pricing page](https://www.testgorilla.com/pricing/) for current plans and prices.

**testee** charges per candidate:

- Generating an interview is free.
- You pay for each candidate who answers at least one question. Invited candidates who never start cost nothing; their credits come back.
- The price per candidate goes down with larger top-ups, your first 3 candidates are free, credits don't expire, and there's no subscription. See [pricing](/pricing).

Which model costs less depends on how many candidates you test in a year and which test types you need. A plan suits steady, high-volume hiring; per-candidate pricing suits occasional hiring. [How to compare test costs](/compare/cheapest-pre-employment-test) shows how to work it out for your own volume.

## Candidate experience

**testee:** candidates see what to expect before they start: that the questions are written by AI, that people at your company review results and decide, and that they can ask for more time or for a review of their result. Each question has its own countdown. Candidates don't see their score or which answers were right. No camera is used. The interview pages show only your company, with your logo and colors if you add them, without testee's header and footer.

**TestGorilla:** on its higher plan, proctoring includes webcam snapshots, disabled copy/paste and full-screen mode ([pricing page](https://www.testgorilla.com/pricing/)). That gives you more monitoring. Whether to use a camera is a trade-off between oversight and candidates' privacy, and camera images are more personal data to protect.

## Where TestGorilla is the better fit

Start from what you need to test. TestGorilla is a better fit when:

- **You want psychometrics.** Cognitive ability, personality and culture tests aren't something testee offers.
- **You need hands-on coding or job simulations.** testee asks code-reading questions, but candidates don't write or run code.
- **You want video answers or AI-led interviews.** testee has neither.
- **You hire in large, steady volumes** where a flat plan works out cheaper.
- **You prefer ready-made tests that have been used many times** over tests written for each role.

## Where testee is the better fit

- **Your role is specific.** A test written from your job description covers what your team actually uses.
- **You hire occasionally.** Paying per candidate avoids a yearly commitment for a few hires.
- **You want to review what's tested** before any question is written.
- **You want each candidate to get their own random set of questions,** so answers are harder to share.
- **You prefer no camera monitoring.** testee uses no video, voice or face analysis.
- **You hire in many languages.** Interviews, the interface and emails work in 23 languages.

## Using both

The two can sit in the same hiring process:

1. **Everyone who applies** takes a short testee knowledge interview written from the job description.
2. **The people who pass** take TestGorilla's cognitive, personality or language tests, or a coding challenge.
3. **People decide,** using both results with the CV and a structured interview.

testee has no contract and its credits don't expire, so adding it to a process that already uses another tool doesn't add a fixed cost.
